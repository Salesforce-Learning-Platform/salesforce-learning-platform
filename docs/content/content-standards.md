# Learning Content Standards

## Purpose

This document defines the baseline standards for learning content maintained in the Salesforce Learning Platform.

The goal is to keep learning material consistent, understandable, maintainable, and useful across different technology domains.

## Content Principles

Learning content should:

- Focus on helping the learner understand concepts, not only memorize syntax or definitions.
- Progress logically from foundational concepts toward more advanced topics.
- Use clear and consistent terminology.
- Explain why a concept matters before introducing unnecessary complexity.
- Include practical context where it improves understanding.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication across learning areas.
- Keep examples relevant to the topic being taught.

## Accuracy

Content must be reviewed for technical accuracy before it is considered complete.

Where a topic depends on a specific platform, framework, library, or version, the content should clearly reflect the applicable context.

Information that becomes outdated should be reviewed and updated rather than left without context.

## Originality and Attribution

Content added to the platform should be written specifically for this repository.

External material may be used for research and verification, but it should not be copied directly into the learning material without appropriate permission or attribution.

When a source, specification, article, documentation page, or other external reference materially contributes to the content, the relevant reference should be identified where appropriate.

## Clarity

Content should use language appropriate to the expected learning level.

Avoid:

- Unexplained jargon.
- Unnecessary repetition.
- Vague instructions.
- Assumptions that the learner already understands a prerequisite that has not been introduced or referenced.

Technical terms may be introduced when necessary, but they should be explained or linked to relevant prerequisite material.

## Practical Learning

Where appropriate, a topic may include:

- Examples.
- Exercises.
- Implementation scenarios.
- Common mistakes.
- Troubleshooting guidance.
- Related concepts or next steps.

Practical material should support the learning objective rather than add complexity without purpose.

## Maintainability

Learning content should be structured so that individual topics can be updated without requiring unnecessary changes across unrelated material.

File names and folder names should remain descriptive and consistent with the repository structure.

Major changes to content organization should be documented when they affect navigation, prerequisites, or relationships between learning areas.

## Completion Standard

A learning topic should be considered ready when:

- Its purpose and scope are clear.
- The content follows a logical progression.
- Technical information has been reviewed for accuracy.
- Examples, where included, support the learning objective.
- References or attribution are included where required.
- The content follows the repository's naming and structural conventions.
