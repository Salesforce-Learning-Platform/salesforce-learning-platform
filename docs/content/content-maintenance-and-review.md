# Content Maintenance and Review Lifecycle

## Purpose

This document defines how published learning content is reviewed, maintained, updated, or retired over time within the Salesforce Learning Platform.

Learning content cannot be treated as permanently complete. Technologies evolve, platform capabilities change, references become outdated, and improvements may be identified through ongoing review.

The maintenance and review lifecycle provides a structured approach for keeping active learning resources accurate, relevant, and useful.

This document applies to published learning modules, documentation, exercises, assessments, projects, learning paths, and supporting resources.

---

## Maintenance Principles

Content maintenance should follow several core principles:

- Published content should remain subject to review.
- Updates should be based on meaningful changes or identified issues.
- The urgency of maintenance should reflect the impact of the issue.
- Significant changes should consider related content and dependencies.
- Outdated content should not continue to appear as current guidance.
- Maintenance activities should remain proportionate to the importance and complexity of the content.
- Historical content may be retained when it provides useful context.

---

## Maintenance Triggers

Content may require maintenance for several reasons.

Common triggers include:

- Changes to a technology or platform.
- Updated recommended practices.
- Incorrect or outdated information.
- Broken links or references.
- Invalid code examples.
- Changes to prerequisites or dependencies.
- Learner or contributor feedback.
- Issues identified during periodic review.
- Changes to related learning paths or assessments.
- Content that no longer aligns with current platform standards.

A maintenance trigger does not automatically require a major revision. The required response should depend on the significance of the change.

---

## Maintenance Categories

Maintenance activities may be grouped according to their scope.

### Minor Maintenance

Minor maintenance addresses limited issues that do not substantially change the learning experience.

Examples include:

- Typographical corrections.
- Broken link updates.
- Small wording improvements.
- Minor clarification.
- Formatting corrections.
- Small updates to examples.

Minor maintenance may result in a patch version update where versioning is applicable.

### Moderate Maintenance

Moderate maintenance involves meaningful improvements while preserving the overall structure and purpose of the content.

Examples include:

- Adding new examples.
- Expanding explanations.
- Updating supporting resources.
- Improving exercises.
- Revising parts of an assessment.
- Updating content for a relevant platform change.

Moderate maintenance may result in a minor version update.

### Major Maintenance

Major maintenance involves significant changes to the learning resource.

Examples include:

- Major restructuring.
- Replacement of outdated concepts.
- Significant changes to learning objectives.
- Substantial technology changes.
- Replacement of exercises or projects.
- A redesigned learning approach.

Major maintenance should consider the impact on related content, learning paths, assessments, and dependencies.

A major version update may be appropriate.

---

## Periodic Review

Published content should be reviewed periodically where continued accuracy is important.

The frequency of review may depend on factors such as:

- The rate of change in the subject area.
- The technical complexity of the content.
- The importance of the learning resource.
- The number of related dependencies.
- The likelihood that guidance may become outdated.

Rapidly changing technical content may require more frequent review than stable conceptual material.

At this stage, the platform does not define a mandatory review schedule.

---

## Review Process

A content review may involve the following stages:

1. Identify the content that requires review.
2. Evaluate the reason for the review.
3. Determine the scope of required changes.
4. Identify affected relationships or dependencies.
5. Update the content where necessary.
6. Perform appropriate quality assurance.
7. Update version information where applicable.
8. Confirm the appropriate lifecycle status.

The process may vary depending on the complexity and significance of the content.

---

## Dependency Review

When significant content changes occur, related resources should be considered.

Affected dependencies may include:

- Prerequisite modules.
- Advanced learning modules.
- Learning paths.
- Exercises.
- Projects.
- Assessments.
- Referenced documentation.
- Supporting resources.

Not every related resource requires modification. The purpose of dependency review is to identify whether changes create inconsistencies or outdated references elsewhere in the learning platform.

---

## Maintenance and Lifecycle Status

Content maintenance is connected to the broader content lifecycle.

A typical maintenance flow may be:

Published

↓

Under Maintenance

↓

In Review

↓

Published

If the content is no longer relevant or continued maintenance is not justified, it may instead move to:

Published

↓

Under Maintenance

↓

Archived

The appropriate lifecycle transition should reflect the actual condition and future status of the learning resource.

---

## Archival Review

Content should be considered for archival when:

- The subject is no longer relevant.
- The technology has been retired or replaced.
- The material has been consolidated elsewhere.
- The content cannot reasonably be maintained.
- A newer learning resource has replaced it.

Before archival, related references and learning paths should be reviewed where practical.

Archived content should be clearly distinguishable from active learning material.

Archiving does not necessarily require deletion. Historical content may remain useful for reference, migration understanding, or documentation purposes.

---

## Reactivation of Archived Content

Archived content may be considered for reactivation when the subject becomes relevant again or when the resource can be updated to meet current standards.

Reactivation should not occur automatically.

Before returning archived content to active use, it should be reviewed for:

- Technical accuracy.
- Current relevance.
- Structural consistency.
- Updated dependencies.
- Applicable learning objectives.
- Content quality requirements.

Depending on the extent of required changes, the content may return to Draft or Under Maintenance before publication.

---

## Roles and Ownership

Each maintained content item should have an identifiable owner or responsible contributor where practical.

The responsible party may be expected to:

- Monitor identified issues.
- Coordinate required updates.
- Support periodic review.
- Maintain lifecycle accuracy.
- Review relevant dependencies.
- Initiate archival when appropriate.

The platform does not define a fixed organizational ownership model at this stage.

---

## Relationship to Versioning

Content maintenance and versioning are related but represent different concepts.

Maintenance describes the process of reviewing and improving content.

Versioning identifies the significance of changes over time.

For example:

- A broken link correction may result in a patch version.
- New learning material may result in a minor version.
- A significant restructuring may result in a major version.

Versioning should follow the principles defined in the Learning Content Versioning Strategy.

---

## Future Evolution

As the Salesforce Learning Platform evolves, the maintenance process may later include:

- Automated review reminders.
- Scheduled content audits.
- Content health indicators.
- Automated broken-link detection.
- Dependency impact analysis.
- Maintenance dashboards.
- Review ownership assignment.
- Automated archival recommendations.

These capabilities are not required at the current stage.

This document establishes the conceptual process for ensuring that learning content remains accurate, relevant, and maintainable throughout its active lifecycle.
