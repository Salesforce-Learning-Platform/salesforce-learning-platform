# Assessment and Knowledge Validation Framework

## Purpose

This document defines the conceptual framework for assessing learner understanding and validating knowledge within the Salesforce Learning Platform.

Learning content should provide opportunities for learners to evaluate their understanding rather than relying only on content consumption. Assessments can help confirm whether learners understand key concepts, can apply knowledge, and are ready to progress to more advanced material.

This framework defines the role and structure of knowledge validation without prescribing a specific technical assessment system.

---

## Assessment Principles

Assessments should support learning rather than exist only as completion requirements.

The framework is based on the following principles:

- Assessments should align with defined learning objectives.
- Questions and activities should measure relevant knowledge or skills.
- Assessment difficulty should be appropriate for the learner level.
- Feedback should support understanding and improvement.
- Assessment results should provide meaningful information about learner progress.
- Knowledge validation should be proportionate to the complexity of the learning content.

---

## Assessment Types

The platform may support different forms of knowledge validation depending on the learning objective.

### Knowledge Checks

Knowledge checks are short activities designed to help learners confirm their understanding of recently covered material.

Examples may include:

- Multiple-choice questions
- True or false questions
- Short-answer questions
- Matching activities
- Concept identification

Knowledge checks are generally intended to reinforce learning and identify areas that may require further review.

---

### Module Assessments

Module assessments evaluate understanding across a broader learning resource.

They may include questions or activities covering multiple concepts introduced within a module.

Module assessments can be used to determine whether a learner has achieved the expected learning objectives before progressing to more advanced content.

---

### Practical Exercises

Practical exercises evaluate a learner's ability to apply knowledge.

Examples may include:

- Writing code
- Configuring a Salesforce feature
- Solving a technical problem
- Debugging an issue
- Completing a structured implementation task

Practical exercises are particularly useful when theoretical knowledge alone is insufficient to demonstrate understanding.

---

### Project-Based Validation

Project-based validation evaluates learning through the completion of a broader practical task.

A project may require learners to combine multiple concepts, technologies, or skills.

For example, a learner may be required to build a small application or Salesforce solution using concepts covered across several modules.

Project-based validation is further defined within the Project-Based Learning Framework.

---

### Final or Path-Level Assessment

A final assessment may evaluate understanding across an entire learning path or major subject area.

This type of assessment may combine:

- Conceptual questions
- Scenario-based questions
- Practical exercises
- Project outcomes

The purpose is to evaluate whether the learner has developed sufficient understanding across the intended learning scope.

---

## Assessment Alignment

Every meaningful assessment should relate to one or more learning objectives.

For example:

**Learning Objective:** Understand the lifecycle of a Lightning Web Component.

**Assessment:** Identify the correct lifecycle hook for a given scenario.

Assessment content should measure the intended outcome rather than unrelated details.

Alignment helps ensure that learners are evaluated on knowledge and skills that the learning resource is designed to develop.

---

## Difficulty Levels

Assessment activities may be organized according to increasing levels of complexity.

### Foundational

Foundational assessments focus on recognition and basic understanding.

Examples include:

- Identifying terminology
- Recognizing concepts
- Recalling basic information

### Applied

Applied assessments require learners to use knowledge in a practical or contextual situation.

Examples include:

- Selecting an appropriate solution
- Applying a concept to a scenario
- Completing a guided technical task

### Advanced

Advanced assessments require deeper analysis, problem-solving, or the combination of multiple concepts.

Examples include:

- Diagnosing a technical issue
- Designing a solution
- Evaluating alternative approaches
- Completing an independent implementation task

Difficulty should reflect the learning level and objectives of the associated content.

---

## Assessment Results

Assessment outcomes should provide meaningful information to learners and, where applicable, to the platform's progress model.

Potential outcomes may include:

- Passed
- Not Passed
- In Progress
- Requires Review

The exact scoring model, passing criteria, and attempt limits are not defined at this stage.

Future platform capabilities may determine how assessment results are calculated and stored.

---

## Feedback Principles

Feedback should help learners understand the outcome of an assessment.

Where appropriate, feedback may include:

- Confirmation of correct understanding
- Identification of incorrect responses
- Explanations of relevant concepts
- References to content that should be reviewed
- Suggestions for further learning

Feedback should support improvement rather than simply indicate whether an answer was correct or incorrect.

---

## Relationship to Learner Progress

Assessment results may contribute to learner progress.

For example, successful completion of an assessment may indicate that a learner has completed a module or satisfied a prerequisite for advanced content.

However, progress tracking and knowledge validation represent separate concepts.

A learner may complete content without passing an assessment, depending on how future platform rules are designed.

The relationship between assessment results and learner progression is defined conceptually alongside the Learner Progress Tracking Model.

---

## Assessment Quality

Assessment content should be reviewed for quality and relevance.

Review considerations may include:

- Accuracy
- Clarity
- Alignment with learning objectives
- Appropriate difficulty
- Unambiguous wording
- Technical correctness
- Relevance to the associated learning content

Assessments should be updated when the underlying learning material changes significantly.

---

## Future Evolution

As the platform evolves, the assessment framework may later support capabilities such as:

- Automated scoring
- Question banks
- Randomized assessments
- Multiple attempts
- Adaptive difficulty
- Practical environment validation
- Assessment analytics
- Skill-based competency tracking
- AI-assisted feedback

These capabilities are not required at the current stage.

This document establishes the conceptual foundation for assessing learner understanding and validating knowledge across the Salesforce Learning Platform.
