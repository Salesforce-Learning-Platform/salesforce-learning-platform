# Core Platform User Experience Principles

## Purpose

This document defines the core user experience principles that should guide the design and evolution of the Salesforce Learning Platform.

The purpose of these principles is to provide a consistent foundation for decisions that affect how learners discover, understand, navigate, and engage with learning content.

These principles are intentionally independent of specific user interface designs, frameworks, components, or technical implementations.

---

## User Experience Principles

The Salesforce Learning Platform should provide a learning experience that is clear, purposeful, and adaptable to different learner needs.

The following principles establish the conceptual foundation for that experience.

---

## Clarity Over Complexity

The platform should make it easy for learners to understand:

- What they are learning.
- Why the content is relevant.
- What they should do next.
- How individual resources relate to broader learning goals.

Information, navigation, and interactions should avoid unnecessary complexity.

Where complexity is unavoidable because of the subject matter, the platform should help learners understand it through structure, context, and progressive learning.

---

## Support Meaningful Progress

The learning experience should help learners recognize their progress over time.

Learners should be able to understand their relationship with:

- Individual learning modules.
- Learning paths.
- Assessments.
- Projects.
- Learning milestones.

Progress should provide useful context rather than becoming a distraction from learning.

The detailed learner progress model is defined separately.

---

## Provide Context

Learning content should not exist as isolated information where meaningful relationships are available.

The platform should help learners understand:

- Prerequisites.
- Related topics.
- Recommended next steps.
- Connections to practical projects.
- Relevant assessments.
- Supporting resources.

Context should help learners make informed decisions about where to continue their learning.

---

## Support Different Learning Approaches

Not all learners will follow the same learning journey.

The platform should support both:

- Structured learning through defined learning paths.
- Exploratory learning through discovery and independent navigation.

A learner should be able to follow a recommended sequence while still having the ability to explore related content when appropriate.

---

## Reduce Unnecessary Friction

The platform should minimize obstacles that do not contribute to learning.

Examples of unnecessary friction may include:

- Confusing navigation.
- Unclear terminology.
- Excessive steps to access relevant content.
- Inconsistent content organization.
- Difficulty understanding progress or status.

Necessary complexity associated with advanced learning should not be removed artificially, but the platform should avoid adding additional complexity through its experience design.

---

## Maintain Consistency

Similar concepts, interactions, and information structures should behave consistently across the platform.

Consistency may apply to:

- Content organization.
- Navigation patterns.
- Terminology.
- Progress representation.
- Content relationships.
- Learning actions.

Consistency helps learners focus on the subject being learned rather than repeatedly understanding how the platform works.

---

## Prioritize Learning Intent

Design decisions should support the learner's current objective.

A learner may be attempting to:

- Begin a new topic.
- Continue an existing learning path.
- Find a specific concept.
- Practice a skill.
- Complete an assessment.
- Apply knowledge through a project.

The platform should provide appropriate paths toward these goals without assuming that every learner has the same intent.

---

## Encourage Practical Application

Learning should extend beyond passive consumption where appropriate.

The platform should support opportunities for learners to:

- Apply concepts.
- Complete exercises.
- Validate understanding.
- Work on practical projects.
- Connect theoretical knowledge with real-world scenarios.

The specific project-based learning and assessment models are defined separately.

---

## Make Relationships Discoverable

The learning experience should help learners discover meaningful relationships between resources.

For example, learners may benefit from understanding:

- Which content should be completed first.
- Which topics build on current knowledge.
- Which assessments validate a particular skill.
- Which projects apply a group of related concepts.

The platform should expose useful relationships without overwhelming learners with unnecessary information.

---

## Support Progressive Learning

Complex subjects should be capable of being approached progressively.

Learning experiences may move from:

Foundational understanding

↓

Core concepts

↓

Practical application

↓

Advanced topics

The platform should support progression without requiring every learner to follow exactly the same path.

---

## Preserve Learner Orientation

Learners should be able to understand where they are within the platform.

The experience should provide sufficient orientation regarding:

- The current learning resource.
- The broader topic or learning path.
- Relevant prerequisites.
- Available next steps.
- Current progress where applicable.

Maintaining orientation becomes increasingly important as the amount of available learning content grows.

---

## Design for Growth

User experience decisions should remain adaptable as the platform evolves.

The platform may eventually support:

- Additional learning domains.
- Larger content collections.
- More learning paths.
- Personalized experiences.
- Different learner profiles.
- Contributor workflows.
- New forms of practical learning.

Current experience principles should therefore avoid assumptions that only apply to a limited amount of content or a single type of learner.

---

## Relationship to Information Architecture

Information architecture and user experience are closely connected but represent different concerns.

Information architecture defines how information and learning resources are organized and related.

User experience principles guide how learners interact with, navigate, and understand that structure.

A well-organized information architecture provides the foundation for a clear and effective learning experience.

---

## Relationship to Platform Evolution

These principles provide guidance rather than fixed implementation requirements.

As the Salesforce Learning Platform develops, specific features and interface patterns may change while continuing to follow the underlying principles defined in this document.

Future capabilities may include:

- Personalized learning recommendations.
- Adaptive learning paths.
- AI-assisted learning support.
- Skill-based experiences.
- Enhanced progress visualization.
- Collaborative learning features.
- Accessibility enhancements.

These capabilities are not defined as current implementation requirements.

This document establishes the foundational user experience principles that can guide future platform design and product decisions.
